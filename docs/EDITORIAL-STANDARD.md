# The enthusiast article standard

The wheel-and-hub walkthrough is the quality benchmark, not a template to paste onto every subject. The requested sitewide revision is in progress. A workflow, a changed introduction, or a populated field does not count as a completed article.

## Teach the promised task

A repair guide explains what the component does, applicable configurations, preparation and tools, how to reach it, ordered removal/service/reassembly, observations that change the next action, and completion checks. Expand “reverse removal” into the supported reassembly actions. Keep replacement-fastener requirements, warnings, tightening sequences, measurements and diagnostic branches next to the relevant work. References support the instructions; they must not replace the very instructions promised by the title.

A diagnostic guide separates symptom, observation, interpretation and next test. Explain the setup, what to inspect or measure, the supported result and what it does—and does not—establish. Never turn a noise, code, or hand-check position into an automatic parts order. A repair is not proven by the absence of an immediately obvious problem.

An overview teaches the component relationships. Connector and wiring articles explain how to read the actual drawing, orientation, identifiers, terminals and supported circuit paths. Specification articles preserve units and applicability and explain how to select the relevant row. These article types do not need fabricated repair steps to look complete.

## Sound like a useful garage companion

Use first-person editorial explanations, ordinary enthusiast language, concrete analogies and occasional profanity. Be warm toward the reader. Aim jokes at frustrating machinery, costly guessing and the work itself. “No parts-cannon bullshit” can make a point; swearing in every paragraph cannot. Do not manufacture personal repair memories or insult people who are learning.

Keep warnings, exact specifications and diagnostic decisions unambiguous. Put humor around the explanation, not inside an instruction where it could obscure an action. Avoid repetitive “the source does not provide” caveats. State real boundaries once at the point where they affect the job, while giving all supported practical help.

## Evidence and review

Only use the configured signed-in Codex tools. No separately billed model API generation is authorized. Private source evidence and intermediate drafts remain under ignored working/data directories. Preserve source identity, engine/equipment scope, numbers, identifiers, tables and diagram associations. Inspect readable original diagrams when a redraw omits or obscures a label; never infer a connection from extracted text order.

Each staged article receives a separate technical review and an editorial read. Schema checks and complete block counts are necessary but do not establish usable instructions. Hold unresolved essential steps, contradictions or illegible evidence rather than publish invented guidance. Independent Codex review is not human mechanic certification.
